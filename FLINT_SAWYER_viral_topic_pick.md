- **The format did not cause the hit.** The source channel's next 10 uploads had a median of about 2,000 views against the hit's 1.88M. That is roughly 940x [NexLev]. Price ratio and the Powerwall name were the same in the hit and the flop, so neither explains it.

The top comment (287 likes) is a price rebuttal. Flint Sawyer can't borrow overclaims. It can borrow the order of the beats and where the limitations go.

## 4. The pick and package

**Pick: original wood window plus storm window versus a replacement window.**
1. It carries variables 1 to 3: a tangible native object, a surprising documented number, and a reframe that the replacement sells new glass when the heat loss comes from air.
2. The anchor stands on four documents (National Trust 2012, NPS Brief 9, ENERGY STAR, Cost vs Value 2025), not on one retail price that commenters can undercut.
3. Timing: early October, when owners of pre-1950 houses get replacement-window pitches before winter.
4. It beats the incumbent by 5 points. The main risk is a crowded but unclaimed search space, which runtime and document depth can answer.
5. Radiators become the next video, teased at the end, which gives the channel a two-part heating series.

### First 30 seconds (79 words, about 30 s at 152 words/min; numbers spelled out for Vidrush)

About two thousand dollars. That is the national average for one new vinyl window, installed, in the twenty twenty-five Cost versus Value report. In twenty twelve, the National Trust for Historic Preservation compared the other option: keep the old wood window and add a storm window. Five cities. Every climate zone they analyzed. The old window came close on energy savings, and it won on return per dollar in every one. The old builders never trusted a single pane.
